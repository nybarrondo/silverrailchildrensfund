Why we never hand a family cash.

Every payment this fund makes goes to a provider or a business, on a child's behalf. It is the single most important rule we have, and it exists to protect your family.

Silver Rail Children's Fund · October 26, 2026

When parents first hear how the fund works, a fair question comes up: why not just give families the money and let them decide? You know your child best. Why put a committee and a payment process between you and what your kid needs?

The answer is that a check made out to a parent can quietly cost a family more than it gives. A check made out to a counselor, a gym, or a camp, on your child's behalf, cannot.

Cash can count against the benefits a family already has

Many Silver Rail families rely on programs that look at household income or resources: the Oregon Health Plan, SNAP, SSI or disability benefits, housing assistance, child-care subsidies, and others. A cash gift from a charity, depending on the program and how it arrives, can be treated as income or as a countable resource. For a family near an eligibility line, a well-meant $500 could mean a reduced benefit, a reporting obligation, or a letter asking for the money back.

We are parents, not benefits counselors, and the rules differ program by program. But the safest path is clear: if money never passes through the family's hands, there is nothing to report and nothing to count. When the fund pays a therapist directly for your child's sessions, the family's income does not change.

Crime Victims' Compensation pays last

Oregon's Crime Victims' Compensation program can cover counseling and other costs for children who were at Silver Rail on October 15. It is a "payer of last resort": before it pays a bill, it asks whether any other source has paid, or will pay, for the same thing. Cash handed to a family for "recovery expenses" can be exactly that kind of other source, and it can reduce or delay what the state program pays.

That is why the fund focuses on the gap nothing else was built to fill, the immediate, trauma-informed play that lets a child regain control and reconnect with simple joy, and on what Crime Victims' Compensation and insurance do not cover: the pottery class, the trampoline gym, the day at the pumpkin patch with classmates, the board games for a quiet weekend at home. Where a family needs help with counseling, we help them apply to the state program first and step in only for what it will not cover, so the two never work against each other.

It protects the fund, too

Every dollar we spend will be published in a monthly ledger. When a payment goes to Bend Rock Gym for twelve day passes, anyone can see what the money did. Nobody has to wonder, and no family ever has to justify how they spent a gift. Direct payment is also what the IRS expects from a charity that supports individuals, and it is the model VictimsFirst, who guide us, has seen work after other tragedies.

How it actually works

  1. You register your child once. One ordinary piece of school paperwork, which we look at and do not keep.
  2. You tell us what would help. A season of soccer, a counselor your child already trusts, a sensory swing for the bedroom. There is no form to prove anything and no wrong answer.
  3. The independent committee hears your request. Three people, none of them Silver Rail parents, say yes or ask a question. The board cannot overrule them.
  4. We pay the provider directly, on your child's behalf. Two officers sign. The provider gets a check or card payment from the fund. You get the confirmation.
  5. It shows up on the ledger as a payment to that business, never with your child's name.

What if the provider needs to be paid up front, or doesn't take checks?

We will work it out with them. We can pre-pay, buy the pass or ticket in the fund's name, or set up an account with the business that your child uses. What we will not do is hand you cash and ask you to pass it along, even for convenience, because the protection only works if the money never becomes yours.

Questions about your own benefits

If you are on OHP, SNAP, SSI, or another program and are unsure whether something we pay for could affect you, email hello@silverrailchildrensfund.org before you ask for it. We can help you check with your caseworker. Nothing we do should ever make a family's situation harder.

This post is general information from volunteer parents, not legal or benefits advice. Program rules change; confirm anything that matters for your household with the program itself.
