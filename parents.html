For Silver Rail parents

What this fund promises your child.

If your child was at Silver Rail Elementary on October 15, this page is for you. It says exactly what we will do, how to register, how to ask for something, and what we keep about your family. Nothing on this page is a sales pitch; it's what we hold ourselves to.

Register your child

The promise, in plain words.

  • Your child is included. Every child enrolled at or present at Silver Rail on October 15, 2026. Their brothers and sisters at home, too, and the children of any adult who was in the building that day. Once you register, there is nothing more to qualify for.
  • You register once. We confirm enrollment one time, privately. Your child stays eligible until their 25th birthday, for as long as this fund exists, at any school, in any town.
  • You tell us what would help. A class, a sport, a camp, a counselor, a day out. One line is enough. You never explain why. For a child who was there, play is how healing starts: quiet, unpressured time where they regain control and reconnect with simple joy. We pay for that as readily as we pay for therapy.
  • We pay the business directly. No reimbursements to chase and no child's name on a check.
  • Group things need no ask. Field trips and activities for all Silver Rail kids are announced to every registered family. Being with two or three classmates somewhere safe and ordinary is often the first thing a child can manage, and it is the kind of support no existing fund was set up to give.
  • We keep almost nothing. Six things about your family (listed below). We never keep the paperwork you show us.
  • We share nothing. Not with the school, not with any other organization.
  • You can leave any time. One email to hello@ and your record is deleted within a week.
  • We pay as funds allow. The balance and every dollar out are published monthly, by category, so you can see it yourself.
  • No one here is paid. And no board member can approve a request for their own child.

This page describes how the fund operates and what families can expect. It isn't a contract: what we can pay for depends on what the fund has, and the board may update these rules as we learn. Any change will be posted here first.

How it works, step by step.

  1. Register below. Your name, email, phone, your child's first name, and a checkbox confirming they were at Silver Rail on October 15.
  2. We confirm enrollment, once. Within three days a Silver Rail parent on the board emails you and asks to see one ordinary thing: a ParentVUE screenshot, the October report card, a class email from the teacher, or the school's own October 15 message to families. We look. We don't save it, forward it, or print it.
  3. You're in. You get a short confirmation email. That's the last time anyone asks you to prove anything.
  4. Ask when something would help. Email hello@silverrailchildrensfund.org or use the form on the home page. "She wants to try the ceramics class at ___, it's $45" is a complete request.
  5. We say yes and pay the business. We aim to answer within three business days and to pay before the activity. Requests over $1,000 need two board members to sign off.
  6. The ledger shows a category and an amount. "Art class, $45." Never a name, never a reason.
Got a letter from the Oregon DOJ about Crime Victims' Compensation? We wrote a plain-language guide to what it covers, how it works with OHP, and the three things to do this week. Read the CVC guide → Or email hello@ and we'll walk through the application with you.

If you've moved and have no paperwork, we'll send you a one-page release that lets the district confirm enrollment to us directly. If your child was present on October 15 but not enrolled, tell us; the board decides those case by case and records the decision without your child's name.

What we keep, and what we never collect.

The six things we keep

  • Your name
  • Your email and phone
  • Your child's first name (grade only if you choose to share it)
  • Whether anyone in your household receives a benefit with an income or asset limit (SSI, OHP, SNAP, TANF, housing) — yes, no, or not sure
  • That you confirmed enrollment on October 15
  • The date we verified it, and who checked

Stored in the fund's own Google Workspace, opened only by named board officers. Never on a personal phone, never exported.

What we never collect or keep

  • Student ID numbers, birth dates, or home addresses
  • The report card or screenshot you show us
  • Medical, counseling, or diagnostic information
  • Any school or district record

If a request you send includes something personal, we pay it and delete the message after. The ledger never carries it.

Before anything else

Get what your child is already owed.

These exist whether or not you register with us. Most take a form and a wait, and nobody hands them to you. If you want help with any of them, email hello@ and a trauma-trained parent volunteer will sit with you and get it filed.

Money and services

  • Oregon Crime Victims' Compensation. Licensed counseling for your child and for you, up to $20,000, after insurance, until age 21. One application per child. Our step-by-step guide, or apply through the Oregon DOJ.
  • VictimsFirst — Make New Memories. A national program, open to any child affected by mass violence, that arranges once-in-a-lifetime experiences. A parent can apply for a child. Learn more.
  • Family Access Network (FAN). Basic needs for Bend-La Pine families: food, clothing, housing help, connection to services. Ask for the FAN advocate assigned to your school.
  • Paid Leave Oregon. Paid time off to care for a child after a serious event. Apply through the state; your employer can't say no.

Step one

Register your child.

One form per child. A Silver Rail parent will email you within three days to confirm enrollment. Nothing you write here is shared.

Your answers go straight into the fund's own private Google Workspace, opened only by board officers. If the form doesn't load, open it in a new tab or email hello@silverrailchildrensfund.org.

Questions parents have asked.

Do I have to ask every time my child needs something?
You ask the fund, not a person: requests go to hello@ and are approved under published rules (up to $250 any officer, up to $1,000 an officer with a note to the board, over $1,000 two officers in writing; never your own child). For individual things, yes, and that's on purpose. You know what your child needs better than we do, and asking is one line in an email. Group activities are open to every registered family without asking.
How much can the fund pay for?
As funds allow. Requests are paid in the order they come in. If the board sets a per-child allowance for a period, it's published before it applies, and we tell you before a request is declined, never after. The balance is public every month.
Who decides what counts?
If it's for your child and a business or provider can be paid for it, it counts. Individual requests are approved by an independent three-person committee with no Silver Rail parents on it. They check that your child is registered and that nobody involved has a conflict. They never ask about your income or whether you need it.
Why do you ask about government benefits?
Because a gift can cost a family more than it gives. SSI, OHP, SNAP, TANF, and housing assistance have income and asset limits. After other school shootings, families received large gifts, had to report them, and lost their benefits. We won't let that happen here. If anyone in your household is on one of those programs, or you're not sure, tell us. The gift is the same; only the container changes — we can hold it for your child and pay providers directly over time, or help you set up a protected account, and we'll connect you with a free legal consult before anything is paid. Your answer is a family record: board officers only, never shared.
What happens to my information if the fund closes?
The registry is deleted before the last dollar is transferred. The receiving nonprofit gets the money, not the list.
Is there a long-term plan?
Yes: a check-in with every registered family each year, support for therapy and activities when other help has moved on, and, as the fund grows, the board intends to explore longer-term support for these kids as they reach adulthood. Our bylaws and this page are the written version of that plan.
